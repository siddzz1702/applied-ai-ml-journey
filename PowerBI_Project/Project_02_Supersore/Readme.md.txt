					 Is Least Profitable Always Unprofitable?
Power BI | Business Intelligence | Data Visualisation

Project Overview

The project analyses the Sample Superstore dataset employing Microsoft Power BI to study product profitability, across the categories and geographical locations, in actual application.

The central business question is:

Are the least profitable products consistently unprofitable, or does their profitability vary across geographical markets?

No question, the dashboard offers an interactive view of category-level performance, the ten least profitable products, state-wise profitability, and quarterly profit trends, in real-world scenarios.

Tools and Technologies

- Microsoft Power BI Desktop
- Power Query (data preparation)
- DAX (data analysis and measures)
- Sample Superstore dataset

Dashboard Components

1. Category Performance
Compares total profit across the product categories and helps spot the relative insight of each category, on the whole.

2. Top 10 Least Profitable Items
Identifies the ten products with the lowest total profit, showing products that may, in practice, need further business investigation, in many respects.

3. Profit by State
Employs a geographical map to compare profitability, across US states and spot regional differences, to a significant degree.

4. Quarterly Profit Trend
Visualises quarterly profit from 2013 to 2016 and includes a linear trendline to show the overall direction of performance, broadly speaking.

5. Department Filter
An interactive slicer is allowing users to filter the dashboard by department.

Interactivity

The dashboard is supporting cross-filtering between its visualisations. Users can, more accurately, pick categories and products to check out their relationship with state-wise profitability and quarterly performance, in many respects.

An extra filtering dimension is being offered by the department slicer.

Business Questions

- What's more, the highest and lowest profits are, in effect, made by which product categories?
- Which ten products have the weakest profitability?
- Does product profitability differ across the geographical locations?
- How has, of course, overall profitability changed over time?
- Are the least profitable products consistently unprofitable across all states?

Key Findings and Business Conclusion
The analysis examines product profitability across categories, individual products, geographical markets and quarterly periods.

The dashboard provides four complementary perspectives:

Category Performance: Compares profitability across product categories to identify stronger and weaker performing segments.

Product-Level Performance: Highlights the ten least profitable products, helping identify items that may require pricing, cost or portfolio reviews.

Geographical Performance: Examines profit distribution across US states to identify regional differences in profitability.

Quarterly Profit Trend: Tracks profitability from 2013 to 2016 and uses a linear trendline to illustrate the overall direction of performance.

Answer to the Central Business Question

Is the least profitable product always unprofitable across every geographical location?
Not necessarily. A product with low overall profitability may perform differently across geographical markets. Its overall profit figure aggregates performance across locations, potentially concealing states where the product is profitable and others where it generates losses.

Therefore, overall product profitability should not be the only basis for business decisions. Product performance should also be evaluated geographically and over time.

Business Recommendations
-Investigate the cost structures and pricing strategies of the least profitable products.

-Identify geographical markets where otherwise weak-performing products generate positive returns.

-Review products consistently generating losses across multiple markets.

-Monitor quarterly profitability to identify changes in performance.

-Use geographical and product-level insights to support inventory, pricing and portfolio optimisation decisions.

Conclusion: Profitability analysis should consider product performance across multiple dimensions rather than relying exclusively on aggregate profit figures.
